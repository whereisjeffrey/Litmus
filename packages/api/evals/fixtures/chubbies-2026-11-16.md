## Approved copy (v3 voice — the benchmark)

**The read (summary):**
> People arrive at your checkout already sold — your brand does that work. On the
> way from "add to cart" to "pay," I found seven moments where some of those sales
> slip away. The two biggest are opened in full below, and both are recoverable
> within a week.

**Floor finding — The $2.98 fee for a promise your product page already made:**
> Charging for return coverage is a legitimate call — apparel returns run 20–30%
> and somebody has to pay for them. The problem isn't the fee. It's that your
> product page says "Easy returns & exchanges within 30 days" — and two clicks
> later the cart asks $2.98 for the same promise. Align the two and this moment
> starts working for you.
>
> The fee arrives pre-selected, explained only by a tooltip, and the way out is a
> button that frames declining as losing something. It then reappears on the
> payment page as "Unlock free returns — $2.98." Three asks, one contradiction.
>
> *What your customer compares you to:* Bonobos' site banner, checked this
> morning: "Free Shipping & Returns in the US." One sentence, no asterisk, no fee.
>
> Unexpected costs surfaced at checkout are the most-cited reason carts die — 48%
> of abandoning shoppers name them (Baymard, 2024). A pre-selected fee is the
> sharpest version of an unexpected cost: it was chosen *for* them.
>
> **The fix:** make one promise and keep it everywhere.
> A · Honest add-on — rename it "Return coverage (optional) — $2.98," unchecked by
> default, with a neutral decline; the product page drops "easy returns" or defines
> it. Copy + one setting · ships this week.
> B · Keep the promise — returns free sitewide, priced into margin, and the
> product-page line stays exactly as written. A pricing decision, not a dev task.

**Strengths (specific, evidenced):**
> Your fit evidence is the best I've walked this month: "96% say true to size"
> right at the size picker, the model's height next to his size, reviewer heights
> on every review. That system removes the #1 reason apparel gets returned —
> protect it. The price living on the Add-to-Cart button is right. And your promo
> field is a quiet "Got a promo code?" link, not an open box — most stores get
> that wrong.
