Six verticals, one anatomy

The walk changes persona and the money path changes name — the finding anatomy doesn't. Each card: the vertical's money path, one steelman-opened finding in its own language. Composite examples for pattern illustration, not live walks.

DTC storecart → paid order

The $2.98 fee for a promise the product page already made

Charging for return coverage is a legitimate call — apparel returns run 20–30%. The problem is the sequence: the product page says “easy returns,” and two clicks later the cart asks $2.98 for the same promise, pre-selected.

Fix: one promise, kept everywhere — rename the add-on and un-check it, or fold returns into margin and keep the page line as written. copy + 1 setting · this week

SaaStrial → paid seat

The card ask, 14 days early

Card-up-front is a real strategy — it filters for intent and lifts trial-to-paid conversion on the accounts that start. The cost sits upstream: the pricing page says “free 14-day trial, cancel anytime,” the signup asks for a card on day zero, and the cancel path runs 4 steps into a chat queue. The promise and the plumbing disagree.

Fix: label it honestly (“free trial · card required”) today; or move the card ask to day 11 with a notice email ahead of it. copy · today ≈1 dev-day

Booking & reservationsinquiry → booked seat

Eleven fields before one open table

Collecting party details up front protects your floor plan and cuts no-shows — a real operational win. But the form asks 11 fields before showing a single available time, and the money question (“is Saturday even possible?”) goes unanswered until field 12. Diners who just want the answer leave with it unasked.

Fix: availability first, details after the slot is chosen — the same 11 fields, resequenced behind a committed diner. form re-order · ≈1 dev-day

Fintech onboardingapplication → funded account

The SSN at step 2, the rate at step 6

Identity verification isn't optional — KYC is the law, and asking is correct. The leak is sequencing and silence: the application asks for the SSN at step 2 of 6, before showing an estimated rate and without one sentence on why it's needed or how it's protected. Applicants aren't refusing the ask — they're refusing the order of it.

Fix: estimated rate from a soft pull first; move full verification behind intent, with the why-we-ask sentence beside the field. resequence · ≈2 dev-days copy · today

Local servicesvisit → booked job

The instant estimate that takes three days

Routing quotes through a callback keeps your estimates accurate — nobody wants to honor a bad instant number. But the form header promises “instant estimate,” and the confirmation page says “we'll call within 2–3 business days.” The homeowner filled the form for the word instant; the next three competitors' tabs are already open.

Fix: keep the callback, change the promise — or show an instant range with the exact number to follow. copy · today range table · ≈half a dev-day

Nonprofitvisit → completed gift

The pre-checked monthly at the moment of generosity

Recurring gifts are the lifeblood — monthly donors are worth several times a one-time gift, and asking is right. Pre-checking the box is the leak: the donor arrived to give once, and the form decided otherwise, with “cover 4% processing” pre-checked beside it. The uplift shows up this month; the disputes, cancellations and unsubscribes show up all year.

Fix: un-check both; make monthly the highlighted, argued-for option (“$15/mo funds a full school year”) instead of the silent default. form settings · today
