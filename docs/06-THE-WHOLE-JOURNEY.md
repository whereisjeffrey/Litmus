# Phase 6 — The Whole Journey: a thought piece

**2026-11-11 · working draft.** This is the big picture docs/05 serves: the complete experience from first touch to year one, why each piece works on a human being, how we gatekeep, and what keeps them coming back. Names (product, themes, tiers) are deliberately deferred — internal systems must work; labels come later. After this doc is agreed, we build it as crude end-to-end wireframes, then tune toward the secret sauce.

**The bar for every surface:** *it talks to you like a $25K consultant — but light.* Short sentences, collapsed by default, drill-in everywhere. The expensive part is the thinking, not the word count. A founder should be able to read the whole first screen in 90 seconds and then fall down exactly as deep a hole as they choose.

**One framing correction we're adopting:** the paid product is *not a dashboard.* Dashboards are walls of numbers you learn to ignore. Ours is a **living report** — a narrative about your site that stays current: findings get re-verified on your live pages, habits get tracked across releases, fixes get confirmed in writing. Analytics tools tell you *what happened*. The living report tells you *what it means and whether you've dealt with it yet.*

## Part 1 — The journey, scene by scene

Dana runs fernway.shop. Eight people, decent traffic, conversion quietly disappointing.

**Scene 0 · Arrival.** Dana installs from the Chrome store — or, better, arrives because a friend sent her a screenshot of *their* drawer ("look what this thing caught on our checkout"). Zero friction holds: no account, no email, nothing between install and first scan. The first ask we ever make of Dana is permission to look.

**Scene 1 · The first 90 seconds (drawer, collapsed).** She scans her product page. What she sees fits one panel height: The Read (three sentences that sound like someone who *gets* retail), two findings — fully expanded, her own pixels ringed inside them, a citation each — and a count row. Everything else is collapsed to single lines. No score, no CTA, no tour. She taps "Show me" and her actual page lights up. **This scene has one job: the sentence "they know my site better than I do."**

**Scene 2 · The itch.** She scrolls. Nine more findings as one-line rows — each located and classified, none explained. The journey strip: Product 9 · Cart 🔒4 · Account 🔒6 · Pay 🔒5 — *"the loudest thing we found isn't on this page."* One locked card shows her own screenshot, clear, no ring: *"We see something here. Do you?"* She stares at her own cart for a full minute. The drawer never interrupts her. **This scene's job: convert trust into curiosity.**

**Scene 3 · The account (free, and before any paywall).** The drawer offers exactly one thing at the bottom: *"Keep this report."* Account creation is positioned as saving *her* artifact, not joining *our* product — the report already feels like hers (her pixels are in it). An account gets her: the report saved, re-checks on the two free findings, and the living report's shell — every locked thing visible as a labeled shape. Why free signup before any sale: the report becomes something she *owns and returns to* (an open loop we hold), and we gain the channel for Scene 6's letters. **Job: possession.**

**Scene 4 · First login (the living report, free shell).** Not a dashboard. It reads top-to-bottom like a consultant's cover letter: the Read, then her site's 3–4 *habits* (our internal clustering; presentation naming TBD) — each a stack: one clear exhibit, the rest blurred crops under sharp captions. Tab 2 is the map: her funnel as screenshot thumbnails of her actual pages, findings pinned, unscanned pages as gray slots. Everything drills: habit → findings → finding → evidence → exhibit. Free Dana can navigate the entire skeleton — she knows exactly how much exists and where. She just can't open most of it. **Job: make the full value *visible* and *navigable* before it's purchasable.**

**Scene 5 · Paying.** No new content is generated at purchase — everything was pre-built, which means unlock is *instant*: blurs clear, rings appear, fixes and rewritten copy fill in, the journey opens, the reasoning behind every verdict unfolds. The purchase feels like removing a blindfold in a room she'd already memorized the shape of. **Job: zero post-purchase deflation — the trailer problem solved by architecture.**

**Scene 6 · The first week (the loop that retains).** Dana ships two fixes. Next morning: *"Re-checked overnight: the shipping total now appears at step 1. Verified. 2 of 11 resolved."* This is the product's emotional core — not our insight but **her competence, confirmed in writing.** She screenshots it to the team channel. Weekly re-walks surface what changed; the megaphone rotates; her remaining count ticks down (or up, when a release regresses — *"your new banner reintroduced the habit we closed in March"*).

**Scene 7 · The quarter and the year.** Habit trendlines across releases. New pages walked as she ships them. Analytics connected: before/after deltas in her numbers on every verified fix — the subscription writes its own renewal case (*"fixes verified this quarter; checkout completion up; here's the letter to forward to your board"*). Quarterly deep re-audits for annual plans. The report never finishes; it keeps being *about now*.

## Part 2 — Why each mechanism works (the psychology, named)

| Mechanism | Principle | Why it bites |
|---|---|---|
| Their pixels inside our cards | Endowment + self-reference | You can shrug off advice; you can't shrug off your own checkout with a ring on it. The report containing *their* material becomes *theirs* — abandoning it feels like losing property. |
| Two findings free, best-first, treatment held | Reciprocity + the doctor model | An unreasonably good free gift creates obligation and sets quality expectations. Diagnosis free / cure paid never feels like a trailer trick. |
| Located-but-unexplained rows | Curiosity gap (information-gap theory) | Specific-but-incomplete is the most compelling information state there is. "Something's wrong with your wishlist" is unfinishable in your head. |
| "Spot it" clear crop | Active participation | The one tease that makes them *work*, on their own property. Effort spent = investment made. (Open design question: the give-up button reveals the ring free, mechanism paid — generosity again — or is the paywall? Leaning reveal-the-ring.) |
| Locked journey counts | Zeigarnik effect | Open loops demand closure. 🔒4 · 🔒6 · 🔒5 is four unfinished sentences. |
| "The loudest thing isn't on this page" | Loss aversion, honestly earned | True (journey findings are structurally paid) and maximally itchy. |
| Exposure language, ranges, citations | Credibility stacking | "Known contributor to abandonment (48%, Baymard)" survives the skeptical CFO in the room. Feelings-speak doesn't. |
| Habit clustering | Narrative compression | Nine findings are a chore; three habits are a story. Stories get retold — retelling is distribution. |
| Verified fixes, dated, in writing | Competence feedback | The deepest retention emotion isn't fear — it's *the product making them feel (and look) effective.* Fear converts; competence retains. |
| Weekly re-walks + rotation + regressions | Variable reward | The report is different every time they return, legitimately. |
| Pre-built locked content | Instant gratification at purchase | The highest-risk moment (post-payment) has zero latency and zero disappointment. |

## Part 3 — The gatekeeping doctrine (consolidated)

**Never gated:** sample quality (the free pair is our best page-local material) · counts · locations · classifications · the skeleton of everything (navigable shapes) · re-checks on free findings · one habit name later, when naming ships.

**Always gated:** mechanisms beyond the pair · every fix and rewrite · sibling instances · the journey's contents · adversary reasoning · habit evidence stacks · trends · their-numbers impact.

**The line test:** *a skeptic inspecting our free tier should conclude we're generous; a customer inspecting our paid tier should conclude the lock was only ever on the details.* If a gate fails either reading, move it. Corollaries: blur imagery, never words; no count or claim that wasn't actually generated; no manufactured urgency anywhere (we literally ship a finding class about that).

## Part 4 — What keeps them coming back, ranked

1. **The verify-fix loop** (the spine — it pays them in competence)
2. Watching: dated re-checks and regression catches ("it's guarding my site")
3. Fresh material: weekly re-walks, rotation, new pages as they ship
4. Habit trendlines across releases (the long story arc)
5. The impact letter in their numbers (the renewal argument, written for forwarding)
6. Team surface: shared fix queue — once three people work from the report, one person can't cancel it

## Part 5 — The proof of concept and its gate

**Build:** the end-to-end experience as crude wireframes (every scene above, one canvas), then a clickable pass with the fernway data; critique quality keeps tuning on Mobbin in parallel. No real pipeline run needed to test the *experience* — the content is already written.

**The "wow" gate — tests before we invest in fine-tuning:**
1. **The unprompted question:** shown Scene 1–2 on a site they know, does a founder ask "what are the other nine?" without being led?
2. **The standup quote:** 24 hours later, can they repeat any finding or habit from memory?
3. **The email:** after Scene 2, do they volunteer an address to keep the report?
4. **The deflation check:** walking Scene 5, does anyone say "that's it?" — if yes, the gate fails where it matters most.

Pass the gate → fine-tune toward secret sauce (copy, pacing, reveal mechanics, naming — all of it). Fail → the structure is wrong and we fix architecture, not adjectives.
